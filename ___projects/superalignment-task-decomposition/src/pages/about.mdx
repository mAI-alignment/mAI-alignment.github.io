**Scalable Oversight by Learning to Decompose Tasks** explores a route to supervising increasingly capable reinforcement-learning (RL) systems: an AI learns to break a hard task into “safe” subtasks that a human-level model, or a human, can reliably judge.

This site is the project's research notebook. It extends the [project page](https://mai-alignment.github.io/projects/superalignment-task-decomposition/) of the [mAI alignment lab](https://mai-alignment.github.io/) with regular [updates](/updates/) on results, methods, setbacks and explanations as the work develops.

## The problem

As AI systems become more capable, people will find it harder to check whether their answers to difficult tasks are correct and aligned with human values. This challenge is known as *scalable oversight*. Directly judging the output of a system more capable than oneself may not be possible, but judging small, well-defined pieces of its work can be.

## Our approach

The project is inspired by [Iterated Amplification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08575), but removes one of its bottlenecks: decomposing tasks by hand. Instead, we train a *decomposer* policy with reinforcement learning to:

- output subtasks in a constrained format,
- keep each subtask within the competence of a weaker language-model executor, and
- maximise the correctness of the final answer once the sub-solutions are recomposed.

## Central questions

- **Part-to-whole generalisation.** Does alignment on subtasks imply alignment of the entire answer? The position paper calls this the *part-to-complete generalization hypothesis* and argues that this generalisation needs to be measured and improved.
- **Robustness to reward hacking.** Can the decomposer be trained without learning to exploit weaknesses in how its subtasks and solutions are judged?
